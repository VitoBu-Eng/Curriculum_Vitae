# Entrepreneurial Experience in Medical Device Development and Startup Building

During my Ph.D., I had the opportunity to be part of an entrepreneurial team working on the development of innovative medical devices, namely T-REMEDIE (Tendon Repair Medical Device) and T-SURE (Tissue Surgical Repair).
Both devices originated from well-established clinical needs reported in the medical literature, as well as from direct input provided by surgeons in Turin. In fact, both entrepreneurial teams included a surgeon who is actively involved in tendon repair and abdominal hernia treatment.  

## T-REMEDIE    
Tendon injuries represent a considerable economic and social burden for both the healthcare system and patients involved. Despite the important advances in biomaterials, surgical techniques, and protocols of post-operative rehabilitation, tendon repair remains a challenge. The number of complications and failures is significant, and a secondary surgery is often required.  
Regarding tendon injury sites in humans, hand, Achilles and rotator cuff tendons are the most prevalent injuries treated by orthopaedic surgeons worldwide. Several sources in the literature report that the incidence rates of these injuries vary broadly within individual countries, with an increase in the number of cases over the years. The *T-REMEDIE* project, for human application, aims to solve the clinical need of hand and Achilles tendon repair. In the veterinary field, the *T-REMEDIE* device is used for equine flexor tendon repair, an injury that commonly affects racehorses and often ends their careers.  

T-REMEDIE is an implantable, biocompatible and biodegradable medical device for tendons repair. The innovative design of the T-REMEDIE device aims to overcome all the issues related to the use of current tendon repair techniques (traditional sutures and other medical devices). The device is used in conjunction with a surgical applicator called T-RESAP (Tendon REpair Surgical APplicator), a non-implantable instrument used to guide the insertion of the T-REMEDIE implantable device into the target tissue, facilitating automatic positioning of the entire system. The device's housing system is interchangeable so that it can be adapted to all configurations of the T-REMEDIE system depending on the size of the tendons.  
Immages of the device are reported here: [**T-REMEDIE project**](https://biomast.polito.it/t-remedie/) and also in [**YouTube**](https://www.youtube.com/@BioMASTLaboratory).

Over the years, the T-REMEDIE device has received approximately **€400k** in funding, including the *Eureka!* Proof of Concept (PoC) grant (€250k) and the *Ancalau award* (€10k).

During several PoCs, the team worked on different aspects related to both the development of the device and the creation of a startup to bring it to the patient’s bedside. In particular:  
1. Evaluation of biopolymers for manufacturing, balancing mechanical properties and degradation time.
1. Identification of manufacturing processes, including Selective Laser Sintering and other additive manufacturing techniques.
1. Design and fabrication of moulds for device production through injection moulding.
1. Development of a business plan and investor pitch.
1. Intellectual Property Strategy and Protection.
1. Interviews and meetings with surgeons.
1. Competitor analysis and data collection.
1. Assessment of the regulatory pathway (MDR and FDA).

Lately, thanks to collaboration with the Department of Veterinary Sciences at the University of Turin, we have evaluated the in vitro cytotoxicity of the biopolymers used in the manufacturing of the device on tenocytes, and performed uniaxial tensile tests to assess the performance of the T-REMEDIE device compared to the 3-Loop Pulley (3LP) suture pattern for equine tendon repair.

The in vitro cytotoxicity assessment was conducted by isolating tenocytes from healthy equine SDFT tendons and exposing them to degradation products of the T-REMEDIE device, collected over a six-month period during degradation in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 36 °C. Tenocyte viability was evaluated after 24 and 48 hours of exposure to degradation products collected at six different time points. The results of this study will soon be published in a scientific journal.  

To evaluate and compare the stability of tendon repair, uniaxial tensile testing was performed, assessing gap formation between tendon stumps at 2 and 3 mm, as well as the failure mode (device, suture, or tendon tissue). Gap formation was measured using graph paper placed in the background and recorded with a professional camera (Canon EOS 1300D). Subsequently, a regularly distributed pattern was manually applied to the tendon surface using a contrast marker to enable analysis via Digital Image Correlation (DIC). This comparison provides preliminary insights into the effectiveness of the device as a potential alternative to conventional suturing techniques.

## T-SURE
Abdominal hernias represent a significant clinical, economic, and social burden, with repair procedures ranking among the fifty most commonly performed surgeries in Italy. Despite advances in biomaterials and the introduction of surgical meshes, recurrence rates remain high (approximately 5–20%), and hernia repair continues to present significant clinical challenges.  
The abdominal wall plays a key role in trunk stability, respiratory mechanics, and pelvic floor function. Its impairment due to primary or incisional hernias can therefore lead to postural issues, breathing disorders, and urinary dysfunction.  
Current repair techniques often fail when physiological loads exceed the strength of the reconstruction, resulting in complications, poor healing, and high healthcare costs. This highlights an unmet clinical need for more effective solutions.  

T-SURE is an implantable device inspired by coil spring geometry. It features a atraumatic insertion tip and a locking system that allows two devices to be connected, enabling hernia closure. Once implanted, it engages a larger volume of tissue compared to conventional sutures, reducing ischemic risk and improving load distribution under increased intra-abdominal pressure. Unlike mesh-based or suture-dependent techniques, T-SURE introduces a knotless, intramuscular approach that eliminates suture-related complications while remaining compatible with existing mesh technologies.

The project has been supported by the Launchpad Proof-of-Concept programme of Fondazione Compagnia di San Paolo, enabling the development and initial testing of the first device and applicator prototypes. A hybrid stainless-steel mould with a replaceable 3D-printed core was also developed, allowing rapid prototyping and iterative design improvements at reduced cost.
